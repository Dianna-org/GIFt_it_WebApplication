# Who to sell it to, how, and how to find out

## Positioning in one sentence
"Make a real-feeling birthday card on a cosy digital craft desk – then download it as a picture or a little animation to send to anyone."

## Who it is for (rank by how reachable they are)
1. **Teens and young adults (14–25)** who like aesthetic/craft content (scrapbooking, journaling, Pinterest, cozy games). Easiest to reach on Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest; they make cards for friends, partners, and group chats.
2. **Kids 10–13 and their parents** – can use it without an account. Reach parents, not kids: family blogs, parenting groups, back-to-school, rainy-day activity lists.
3. **Teachers and youth workers** – a free classroom activity (birthday cards for classmates, thank-you cards). Reach via teacher communities and school newsletters.
4. **Adults who forgot a birthday** – last-minute gifters. Reach with search ("make a birthday card online free") and Pinterest.

## What to sell (everything must cost you nothing to start)
- **Free core app.** Growth first.
- **Later, optional income:** premium sticker/tape packs, more portfolio slots, extra export layouts (seasonal: Christmas, Mother's Day), "no watermark" exports. If you add a free-version watermark, make it small.
- **B2B later:** classroom licence; HR teams making team cards.
- Don't add ads before you have users; they cost trust with a young audience (and have extra rules for under-18s).

## How to find out if people want it (do this before building more)
1. Show the 20-second screen recording to 10 people in each segment; ask "would you use this for the next birthday?" and "what would you pay, if anything?".
2. Put the live link in one Instagram story poll: "Which sticker pack next?".
3. Track three numbers weekly: visitors, % who download a card, % who come back within 14 days. If the middle number is low, fix the experience before marketing harder.
4. Add privacy-friendly analytics (e.g. self-hosted Plausible/Umami, free to run) – never record the cards themselves.

## Channels and what to post
| Channel | Cost | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram Reels / TikTok / YouTube Shorts | free | 15–30 s time-lapses of a card being made, "satisfying" tape-cutting close-ups, before/after, GIF results. Same video on all three. |
| Pinterest | free | Pin each exported card with the title "DIY birthday card ideas"; links back to the app. |
| Reddit / Discord craft & journaling communities | free | Share as a maker ("I built a cozy card maker"), follow each community's self-promotion rules. |
| Schools / teachers | free | One-page "5-minute classroom activity" PDF. |
| Product Hunt / university showcase | free | Launch day with a short demo video. |
| SEO | free | A landing page for "free online birthday card maker", "make a birthday card for a friend". |

**Built-in virality:** the exported pictures/GIFs can carry a small "made with GIFt it!" corner mark (let users remove it later as a paid perk) so every shared card is an ad.

## Pricing sanity check (when you get there)
Compare free competitors (Canva, Adobe Express, Piktochart etc.) honestly: you will not beat them on templates. You win on **feel** (the 3D desk, real tool behaviour, animations). Sell the feeling.

## Legal basics before a public launch
- Privacy policy and terms (what you store: email, display name, birth date, cards).
- Cookie/storage notice if you add analytics.
- Age rules (see CI-CD-AND-DEPLOYMENT.md §5).
- A way to delete an account and all its cards on request (not built yet – add `DELETE /api/me`).
